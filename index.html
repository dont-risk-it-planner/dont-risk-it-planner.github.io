Don’t Risk It!

Trajectory-Conditioned Distributional Risk for LiDAR-Only Crowd Navigation

Abstract

Efficient crowd navigation requires anticipating dynamic collision-free space without relying on fragile object tracking. We propose the Don’t Risk It Planner (DRIP), an end-to-end LiDAR-only planning framework that couples occupancy forecasting with trajectory-conditioned clearance and uncertainty estimation. Operating directly on LiDAR-derived occupancy grid histories, DRIP predicts a distribution over minimum robot–obstacle clearance for candidate control sequences to evaluate collision risk. The model is trained using a risk cost that grounds predictive uncertainty directly in empirical collision outcomes rather than geometric regression alone. In closed-loop experiments with up to 45 pedestrians, DRIP achieves higher success rates with fewer collisions and reduced freezing behavior compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

Method

Prediction errors matter differently depending on the trajectory under consideration: an occupancy error far from the robot’s path may be irrelevant, whereas a small error near its closest encounter with an obstacle can change whether the trajectory should be chosen. DRIP therefore assesses each candidate through its distributional collision risk. A distribution over the minimum clearance along the trajectory is predicted from the LiDAR history, and the extent to which this distribution violates the required safety buffer is quantified as a single risk value.

  • LiDAR-only predictive planning. Detection- and tracking-free occupancy forecasting is coupled with a trajectory-conditioned distribution over minimum clearance, which is mapped to collision risk with a squared-MMD functional.
  • Risk-calibrated uncertainty learning. Supervision from safe and unsafe trajectories shapes the predictive variance through its downstream effect on the risk estimate, rather than through scene-level forecasting accuracy alone.
  • Sampling-based planning. The risk, a next-step occupancy check, goal progress and smoothness are combined in a DWA-style local planner that replans at 10 Hz.

Overview of DRIP’s LiDAR-only prediction and trajectory-conditioned risk architecture. A history $\mathcal{H}$ of pose-warped BEV occupancy maps is encoded independently by a shared-weight spatial CNN, conditioned on ego velocity $\mathcal{V}$ through FiLM. Spatiotemporal Transformer blocks then reason over the temporal history and spatial BEV tokens to produce a shared scene representation $z_{scene}$. Two branches use this representation: the occupancy decoder predicts the future evolution of the scene ($\widehat{\mathbf{O}}_k$) over a 20-step horizon, while the trajectory-conditioned Distance Head combines scene features with candidate trajectory queries $\mathbf{q}_k$ to predict a stepwise clearance distribution $(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)$ and a trajectory-specific kernel width $\lambda$. These predictions are subsequently used to compute the distributional $\mathrm{MMD}^2$ risk $\widehat{r}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{u})$ for each candidate trajectory $\mathbf{u}$.

Real-World Deployment

We deploy DRIP on two platforms with different form factors and sensing: a Pioneer 3-DX with a 2D RPLIDAR A3, and a full-scale autonomous wheelchair with a Livox Mid-360 3D LiDAR whose returns are filtered, ground-segmented and projected into 2D BEV occupancy grids. The whole perception and planning pipeline runs onboard.

Across 20 closed-loop trials (paths of 15–35 m) in a cluttered laboratory space and an academic corridor, with unscripted humans as dynamic obstacles, DRIP succeeds in 17 of 20 runs (85%) without human intervention. In open corridors it commits to wide bypasses well before mutual deceleration; in tight bottlenecks the clearance floor keeps the base off the walls while the learned risk moderates speed through moving crowds, avoiding freezing.

Simulation Benchmarks

We evaluate in the PedSim simulator (Lobby world) with a differential-drive Turtlebot2 and a 270° 2D LiDAR, against DWA, TEB, SCOPE and DRL-VO, with 35 and 45 active pedestrians (100 nominal runs each) and with Gaussian LiDAR range noise of $\sigma = 0.05\,\mathrm{m}$ (50 runs each). A run succeeds if it reaches the goal without collision within 90 s. DRIP, DWA, TEB and SCOPE use raw 2D LiDAR only; DRL-VO† is given privileged pedestrian tracks and velocities, and is therefore not evaluated under noise.
